Epistemic Uncertainty
in Intelligent Systems

VTU Belagavi · Computer Science Engineering · 2023–present
Studying the representation, propagation, and failure of uncertainty in learned systems.

How is uncertainty represented, propagated, and lost in intelligent systems? What structural failures emerge when complex uncertainty is compressed into simplified predictions?

My research develops information-theoretic and geometric frameworks for understanding epistemic uncertainty in sequential decision-making systems. The work connects theory to practice through open-source infrastructure, peer-reviewed publications, and rigorous empirical investigation.

Current focus: Latent-state uncertainty in sequential systems—world models, state-space systems, retrieval architectures, and failure diagnostics for learned representations.

Research Architecture

The research program is organized around three foundational pillars. All applications—robotics, vector databases, LLMs, finance, cybersecurity, scientific systems—are testbeds for investigating these core pillars, not separate research directions.

Representation

How should uncertainty be represented without losing essential information?

  • Simplex Collapse
  • Measure Gap
  • Identifiability (ICC)
  • AMBIVERT
  • Transformation-Stable Information

Propagation

How does uncertainty evolve through time and decisions?

  • Action-Conditioned Ambiguity World Models
  • World Action Models with Epistemic Uncertainty
  • Predictive Failure Hitting-Times
  • GIRL (Hallucination Control)
  • Regret-Aware Policy Optimization

Failure & Diagnostics

What failures emerge when uncertainty is lost or misrepresented?

  • Calibration Drift
  • Constraint Drift in LLMs
  • Silent Failures in Systems
  • Sensitivity-Uncertainty Alignment
  • HED (Early Detection Score)

Testbeds: Robotics · Vector Databases · Large Language Models · Finance · Cybersecurity · Scientific Systems

Long-Term Vision

Over the next decade, this research program aims to build the theory, systems, and software infrastructure required for intelligent systems to reason under epistemic uncertainty without losing critical information.

The immediate priority is establishing foundational theory around representation and propagation, building a production-grade open-source ecosystem, and demonstrating empirical impact across diverse application domains. By 2030, the goal is to have created a recognized subfield with clear standards, metrics, and practices for uncertainty-aware system design.

This work will eventually mature into a research group with deep collaborations across academia and industry, dedicated to making epistemic uncertainty a first-class concern in intelligent systems engineering.
